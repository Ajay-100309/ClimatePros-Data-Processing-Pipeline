How good is the Parts Finder AI?
A plain-language summary of the September 2026 evaluation

What this system does

When a technician finishes diagnosing a broken unit, they type what they found into the mobile app - something like "bad condenser fan motor". The AI then searches a library of 15,391 past service jobs, finds the ones that describe the most similar problem, looks at which parts were actually used to fix those jobs, and suggests up to 6 parts the technician will probably need. Think of it as a colleague who remembers every job the company has ever done.

How we tested it

We took 1,000 past jobs where we already know which parts were really used. For each one, we hid the answer, showed the AI only the problem description, and checked: did any of the parts the technician actually used appear in the AI's top-6 suggestions?

We asked the question three different ways, because how you phrase it matters:

PhrasingExampleWhy we tested it
A. Full write-up"Disassembled and found one of two condenser fan motors is bad."The cleanest possible question - our benchmark
B. Raw ticket text"12926703 - Severity 3 - Created 05/18... NTE: 1250..."What happens if someone lazily pastes the work order
C. Short tech phrase"bad condenser fan motor replaced"What a real technician will actually type on a phone

On top of that, a second, independent AI reviewed a sample of 200 searches and graded whether each past job the system pulled up was genuinely about the same problem - a quality check on the search itself, not just the final answer.

So the AI finds a right part roughly every second job, clearly beating the best non-AI shortcut. About 1 in 4 jobs has a correct part in the very first suggestion.

The good news

Typing a short phrase works just as well as writing full sentences. The short technician phrasing scored 52% - statistically identical to the perfect write-up. The mobile app's simple free-text box is the right design; technicians don't need to write essays.
The math behind the ranking is already as good as it can get. We tried every variation of the scoring recipe (how many past jobs to look at, how much weight to give popular parts, different weighting formulas) - they all land within 1 point of each other. We also confirmed, on 50 sampled jobs, that this evaluation's calculations match the live service's code exactly, suggestion for suggestion.
The search quality filter works. The judge AI confirmed that 81% of what the system retrieves is at least a related problem, and that matches below the system's similarity cutoff are mostly junk (64% unrelated) - exactly what the cutoff is there to remove.

The honest weak spots

Raw ticket text ruins the results - 52% drops to 28%. The work-order text is full of tracking numbers, dates and billing codes that confuse the search. The app must always ask the technician what they found, and never fall back to the ticket description. (The app is already built this way - this test proves why it must stay that way.)
The AI is great at common parts and nearly blind to rare ones. When the needed part is one of the 10 most-used parts, it scores 87%. When it's a rarely-used part, it scores just 7%. In other words, the AI is strongest exactly where an experienced tech needs the least help. This isn't a flaw in the math - rare parts simply have too few past examples.
The library is still too small - that's the real ceiling. In 1 out of 3 test jobs, no past job in the library used the right part and described a similar problem, so no scoring formula could have found it. Today only 2,606 of the 15,391 indexed jobs have parts recorded against them.

This pattern makes sense: fan motors and compressors fail the same way everywhere, so past examples transfer well. Wiring problems and drain blockages are one-of-a-kind and often need no stocked part at all.

What should happen next

  1. Keep growing the library. Every batch of processed dispatches adds searchable history, and history - not smarter math - is what raises the score. Jobs with recorded parts are worth the most; today only ~17% of indexed jobs have any.
  2. Re-run this evaluation after major growth. It is one command (venv/bin/python analysis/10_rag_eval.py) and reuses everything already computed, so a refresh takes minutes, not hours.
  3. No scoring changes needed. The current live configuration (look at 100 similar jobs, small popularity bonus) is confirmed as good as any alternative we measured.
What this test did not cover: the live service also runs a gatekeeper AI that rejects nonsense input (e.g. random letters) before searching - that gate wasn't part of this test. And the scores above measure suggestions against the parts technicians recorded - where past technicians forgot to log parts, a "correct" suggestion can still be counted as a miss, so the true usefulness is, if anything, slightly better than these numbers.
